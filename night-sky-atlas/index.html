A NOTE FROM THE OBSERVATORY

Take this atlas outside.

Start with a recognisable pattern, then identify one star at a time. The chart is a reference illustration; the observation instrument estimates a direction for a chosen star. For a real observing session, check cloud cover and find a safe, dark place with a clear view of the horizon.

Numerical reference: Sidereal time is checked against the published 1987-04-10 00:00 UT example in Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (GMST 13h 10m 46.3668s). This validates one benchmark, not the star catalogue or the full observational model. HOW TO READ THE ATLAS

The chart uses rounded reference equatorial coordinates (RA and Dec, approximately J2000) projected onto a flat rectangular diagram, with right ascension increasing to the left. Each constellation has its own scale. Lines are conventional visual guides, not physical connections. The charts are not live sky views and do not account for your date, time, location, horizon, or precession. Seasonal guidance assumes mid-northern latitudes and evening viewing. Coordinates are approximate educational values, not astrometric measurements.

To verify star positions and identifiers: SIMBAD. Scientific audit status (Public Beta 1.0): catalogue positions are illustrative approximations and have not been independently cross-matched against SIMBAD; the horizon compass now hides below-horizon targets. For general skywatching: NASA Skywatching.
