Vermilion.

A brilliant, hazardous red

ORIGIN / HISTORY / CULTURE✳

Vermilion is a red pigment based on mercury sulfide (HgS), also found naturally as cinnabar. It has a long history in painting, lacquer and decorative arts across different regions.

Its brilliance came with a serious cost: mercury compounds require careful handling. The historical pigment should not be confused with every modern paint sold under the name “vermilion.”

Cochineal.

A color made by insects

ORIGIN / HISTORY / CULTURE✳

Cochineal dye is produced from scale insects cultivated on prickly pear cacti, especially in the Americas. The dye became a significant commodity in early modern global trade.

It can color textiles directly and can also be used to prepare lake pigments for painting. The hue varies with processing, mordants and surrounding chemistry.

Madder Lake.

A plant becomes paint

ORIGIN / HISTORY / CULTURE✳

Madder plants supplied red dyes for textiles for many centuries. By precipitating colorants onto a substrate, makers could prepare lake pigments for painting.

A lake pigment is not the same material as the original dye bath. Recipes and resulting shades varied considerably.

Earth transformed by heat

Lead white, a basic lead carbonate pigment, was widely used in European painting for centuries because of its working properties in oil paint.

Its toxicity has driven restrictions and substitution. A pale screen swatch cannot reproduce the texture, opacity or handling qualities of the actual material.

COLOR VALUE#E4DCCB
